26 UI Design Style

Material Design 2 Classic Material

Google's original paper-and-ink system — realistic elevation and soft shadows, bold color, Roboto, the floating action button and ripple feedback.

Introduced: 2014 (Material)Mature · huge ecosystemStandard for Android & web for years

👁At a glance

Material Design 2 is Google's original Material system (launched 2014, later called “Material 2”) built on a metaphor of paper and ink: surfaces carry realistic elevation through soft shadows, bold primary and accent colors sit on a baseline grid, and the Roboto typeface anchors the type scale. It introduced signature patterns like the floating action button (FAB), ripple touch feedback and meaningful, choreographed motion. It standardized Android and web UI for years and is still widely implemented today through libraries such as MUI (Material UI) and Angular Material. It precedes Material 3 / Material You, which added dynamic color.

⚖️Strengths vs Limitations

Presented neutrally. Weigh them against your project's goals.

✅ Strengths

  • Mature and exhaustively documented
  • Huge ecosystem (MUI, Angular Material)
  • Strong built-in accessibility
  • Familiar and consistent across platforms
  • Fast to build with ready-made components

⚠️ Limitations

  • Can look dated next to M3 / dynamic color
  • Strong shadows can feel heavy
  • Reads unmistakably as “Google”
  • An opinionated system with firm rules
  • Less personalization than Material 3

🎯When it fits / when to avoid

👍 Good fit when

  • Android and web apps
  • Teams already using MUI or Angular Material
  • Enterprise and productivity products
  • When a proven system beats novelty

👎 Avoid when

  • Brands needing a distinctive custom look
  • When M3 / dynamic color is preferred
  • Ultra-minimal or flat aesthetics
  • iOS-first native apps
