31 UI Design Style

Bauhaus

Form follows function — geometric primitives, primary colors, strict grids and clean sans-serif type from the most influential school in modern design.

Origin: 1919–1933 (Germany)Foundational · modernistBackbone of geometric branding

👁At a glance

Bauhaus was the influential German art and design school (1919–1933) whose style champions “form follows function”: geometric primitives (circle, square, triangle), a primary-color palette (red/yellow/blue) plus black and white, mathematical grids and asymmetry, and clean sans-serif type. A foundational modernist language still used in branding, posters and geometric UI.

🧬Key characteristics

Geometric primitives

Circle, square and triangle as the core visual vocabulary

Primary color palette

Red, yellow and blue plus black and white — nothing else

Grid & asymmetry

Mathematical grids with deliberate, balanced asymmetry

Function-first modernism

Clean sans-serif type; ornament removed in favor of purpose

⚖️Strengths vs Limitations

Presented neutrally. Weigh them against your project's goals.

✅ Strengths

  • Timeless, credible and iconic visual language
  • Strong, bold visual identity that stands out
  • Systematic and scalable across a design system
  • Pairs well with minimal, uncluttered UI
  • High-contrast, accessible primary palette

⚠️ Limitations

  • Can feel austere or academic
  • Primary palette limits emotional mood
  • Geometric rigidity constrains layouts
  • Less warmth and personality than softer styles
  • Risk of pastiche — looking like a cliché homage

🎯When it fits / when to avoid

👍 Good fit when

  • Brand / identity, posters and editorial work
  • Culture, arts and education projects
  • Geometric UI systems and design languages
  • Bold, minimal brands wanting a strong stance

👎 Avoid when

  • Soft / playful or luxury-emotional brands
  • Imagery-led, photographic experiences
  • A non-modernist, distinctive look is needed
  • A warm, approachable consumer tone is the goal
